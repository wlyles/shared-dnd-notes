# Character Building Primer
Read this for all the info you need to build your character!

## Setting
This campaign will be set in the Forgotten Realms, so most anything you know about the “normal” D&D setting will be applicable here. This campaign will largely take place in the Moonshae and/or Nelanther Isles and the oceans surrounding them. Aside from these islands you *might* also visit other islands in the Trackless Sea or some parts of the Sword Coast such as Waterdeep, but this content will mostly cover the islands where the majority of the campaign will be set.
## The Moonshae Isles
If you want more info about the Moonshae Isles, read [the lore dump](./the-moonshae-isles.md). Otherwise, this is the high-level lore:

The Moonshae Isles are a collection of islands located west of the Sword Coast where the waters of the Sea of Swords give way to the Trackless Sea. The islands are generally untamed and wild, known for their dark forests, towering mountains, and ancient standing stones left by the fey creatures who inhabited the island in ancient history. The Moonshaes are primarily inhabited by the human societies of the Ffolk and the Northlanders, who have historically been at odds with one another. The fey creatures that were once plentiful have largely been driven out through the Moonwells which are guarded by druids, though sightings of fey creatures are still fairly common.

The game will be set in the year 1371 DR just after the formation of the United Moonshae Isles under High Queen Alicia Kendrick. This follows a period of unrest and infighting among the various peoples that laid claim to the islands, so there are still pockets of resistance to the High Queen’s rule and piracy abounds in the area. Additionally, three years ago in 1368 the wardens of the druid groves in Myrloch Vale began to disappear upon receiving summons to enter the moonwells, weakening the barrier between the Material Plane and the Feywild and allowing fey creatures to enter into the Moonshae Isles.

The Nelanther Isles are located in the southern part of the Trackless Sea and consist of nearly one thousand islands, a majority of which lack potable water and are thus uninhabitable. The rest of the islands which can support life are home to ruthless pirates who prey upon ships from Amn, Tethyr, Calimshan, the Sword Coast, and the Moonshae Isles. There were very few permanent settlements on these islands due to the harsh environment and difficulty in navigating many sections due to natural phenomena such as whirlpools and high winds, and pirates often lived on their ships or in ramshackle huts. The largest permanent settlement is the port of Skaug, home to large dockyards capable of welcoming as many as forty ships and defended by the Skaug Corsairs. Two years before the campaign begins the port was terrorized by a juggernaut formed from shipwrecks by wild magic in an event known as Halaster’s Higharvestide, which some areas of the city have not fully recovered from.
## Who… Are You?
We will start the campaign in Waterdeep, though you will not be there long. You'll ~~probably~~ be ~~level 1, but I'm considering up to~~ level 3 ~~or even staggered levels where each player chooses the level they think they'd be based on past experience~~. You will all be boarding a merchant vessel called the Gleaming Gander on the morning of its departure, and it’s up to you to decide why you’re there.

The Gleaming Gander is a carrack measuring 65 feet in length with a crew of 40, with three decks and three masts. The middle deck contains the crew and passenger quarters as well as the cannons, while the lower deck is the cargo hold and brig. The ship is notable for the large gilded figurehead of a goose with wings spread wide. Although somewhat small for a carrack, this was a boon for a ship sailing the perilous waters of the Moonshae Isles, allowing the ship to more easily traverse narrow straights and evade pirates. Because of this the Gleaming Gander boasts a flawless record of never losing cargo in any of her previous journeys to the Moonshae Isles, something which no other ship can boast. This is doubly impressive considering the rather light armaments - just four 90mm bombards below decks and a 50mm culverin on a swivel on the poop deck.

The Gleaming Gander will be leaving Waterdeep to engage in the first trade mission from the mainland to the newly formed United Moonshae Isles, so the ship’s departure is a big event in Waterdeep and you’ll have a crowd to see you off. The ship’s primary mission is one of trade, carrying goods ranging from bare necessities to the finest luxuries.

There are many reasons you might be on the Gleaming Gander for this voyage. Here are some ideas to get you started, but feel free to come up with your own!

- Maybe you’re a member of the crew. You can be anyone except the captain or the first mate, which includes any of the following roles:
    - Second mate (typically responsible for navigation)
    - Third mate (typically responsible for safety of the crew)
    - Bosun or bosun’s mate (shortened from “boatswain”, the bosun is the most senior position in the deck department responsible for the ship’s hull)
    - Surgeon or surgeon’s mate (an archaic term in Faerun since proper surgery is rarely necessary when you have healing spells)
    - Carpenter or carpenter’s mate (responsible for maintaining the ship’s wooden parts - which is most of it)
    - Gunner or gunner’s mate (responsible for maintaining the ship’s weapons and ammunition)
    - Cook
    - Chaplain
    - Quartermaster (responsible for managing ship finances and stocks)
    - Helmsman (the person steering the ship)
    - Stewards (4 total - responsible for keeping the ship clean)
    - Seamen (8 total - low-ranking crewmembers performing general tasks on the ship)
    - Topmen (8 total - seamen who work the rigging to furl and unfurl sails)
    - Midshipmen (4 total - low ranking crewmembers training to become naval officers)
- Maybe you’re one of the merchants on the ship hoping to exchange your goods.
- Maybe you’re a mercenary hired to protect the ship in case of pirate attacks.
- Maybe you’re a traveler who has booked passage on the ship.
- Maybe you’re a prisoner being extradited to the Moonshae Isles and are being held in the brig.
- Maybe you’re a stowaway hoping to escape your past and start a new life in the Moonshae Isles
- Or something else, these are just ideas to get you started!

### Available Character Creation Options
If it’s in officially printed 5th edition content, you can use it! If you’re picking something that’s not from the 2024 PHB, let me know because it might need tweaking, but you won’t be prevented from using it. No Unearthed Arcana content unless otherwise specified.
### Firearms
Of course there are guns. IDK if guns would be around in 1371 DR lore-wise but what good is a campaign with pirates if they can’t have guns? The 2024 PHB includes firearms now, but we’re going to make some slight tweaks to make them a little more fun to play with. Proficiency with firearms is not always well defined, so if your class doesn’t get proficiency and you think it should, let me know. Additionally, pistols are weaker than printed but gain the light property because dual wielding with pistols is cool. All of these weapons are blackpowder weapons similar to what you’d see in the Renaissance era. Here are the stats:

### Other Notes
There are a few things I don’t like about the 2024 rules, so the following house rules will be used to make tweaks to the 2024 ruleset:
- We will be applying the 2024 errata described below. It’s one page, please at least skim it to see if anything applies to stuff you’d like to use: https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/errata/PHB-24/PHB-2024_v1.pdf 
- The Gleaming Gander has a quartermaster who keeps his stocks well supplied. You’ll be able to buy basic necessities from him while on the ship, including most basic spellcasting components.
- The Crafter origin feat will not give a 20% discount on all non-magical items. If you want a discount, you have to get it the old fashioned way by haggling
- If you peek at the rules glossary you will see that the Hide action now has a set DC of 15. This will not be applied, and Hide check DCs will be appropriate for the circumstance. Generally speaking, you can expect that the average DC is probably 15, but often it will be opposed by Perception instead. Either way I’ll give you hints to gauge the difficulty before you commit.
- Limitations on the number of leveled spells you can cast per turn are dumb. Cast whatever you want and watch those spell slots disappear!
- And as a reminder, the rules as written state that natural 20s and 1s are only automatic results for attack rolls, and I stick to this rule. I don't use the common house rule that these are automatic results on skill checks. There are some things that experienced characters should be able to do without even thinking, and there are some things that no amount of luck can help an inexperienced character with. Instead, a natural 20 or 1 will be the best or worst outcome possible, which is typically at least a partial success/failure.

Because this is my first time playing with the 2024 rules please be understanding if we need to make additional changes on the fly. Also, I haven’t internalized all of the new rules yet, so if you notice I’m doing something wrong please let me know!

## Optional Stuff
You don’t have to read the rest of this, it’s mostly suggestions for character building if you want to look through character options that I found relevant. Don’t limit yourself to these suggestions, but if you want some inspiration take a look!
### Particularly Thematic Character Building Options
If you’re not sure what you want to do with your character yet or looking for something especially aquatic-themed, here are some options to consider. You don’t have to pick anything from this list if you don’t want to, but many of these options are thematically appropriate and may have advantages in this campaign setting (such as a swim speed or water breathing). Note that you will not be overly punished for not having such features, and your characters will have reasonable access to things which can help them overcome these limitations if necessary.
#### Subclasses

**Artillerist (Artificer)**: Artillerists use arcane cannons to bombard enemies in combat. Many artillerists find work as ships’ gunners or gunner’s mates.

Also, if you’re going to play an Artificer, we’ll use the Unearthed Arcana version here: http://dnd2024.wikidot.com/artificer:main . We’ll be adding firearms to the list of weapon proficiencies, and if we wind up reaching level 11 we’re also going to change spell-storing items so that they can only store spells up to second level like they used to (which avoids absurd power creep on the wizard)

**Circle of the Stars (Druid)**: Stars are important to navigation, and nobody knows them better than druids of this circle. Most commonly seen on the high seas assisting with navigation.

**College of Swords (Bard)**: One of two classes which is the stereotype of the rakish, debonair swordsman. Not as strong as BG3, which is a good thing. Also, because bards lost some weapon proficiencies in 2024, this subclass will also grant shortsword, rapier, longsword, hand crossbow, and pistol proficiencies (in addition to medium armor and scimitar proficiency).

**Swashbuckler (Rogue)**: The other stereotype. Compared to the bard, trades magical and support abilities for more of a focus on duelling. Note that the rogue proficiencies include our updated pistols which are light weapons.

**Circle of the Sea (Druid)**: A new subclass for 2024. You can draw on the tempestuous forces of oceans and storms. Notable for their ability to use wild shape to manifest ocean spray around them to damage and push nearby enemies away. Also grants a swim speed.

**Tempest Domain (Cleric)**: Controls the powers of storms, seas, and skies. Notable for their ability to maximize thunder or lightning damage rolls a few times a day.

**Storm Sorcery (Sorcerer)**: Vaguely similar to tempest cleric, but as a sorcerer. This class is also explicitly called out in its description as useful to a ship’s crew for their ability to manipulate the weather, so storm sorcerers often hold positions as officers.

**Storm Herald (Barbarian)**: Your rage is fueled by primal magic which taps into forces of nature. One of the choices as the source of this power is the sea. These kinds of warriors are common among Northlanders.

**Fathomless (Warlock)**: Your pact is with an entity of the ocean, the Elemental Plane of Water, or another otherworldly sea. This might include a kraken, an ancient water elemental, a kuo-toa hallucination, a merfolk demigod, or a sea hag (!!!). They can summon a spectral tentacle to control the battlefield and can gain a swim speed and water breathing.

**Beastmaster (Ranger)**: You can choose a sea creature as your companion


#### Species
None of these are in the 2024 rules and thus might need tweaks to get them in line with the new species, so let me know if you’re interested first. These are just summaries, and the final abilities might not be exactly what you see here.

**Water Genasi**: You are descended from marids: aquatic genies from the Elemental Plane of Water. Comes with a swimming speed, water breathing, acid resistance, and some free water-based spells.
Triton: Humanoids with blue skin, webbed hands and feet, and fins. Mechanically very similar to a water genasi, but with resistance to cold instead of acid, different spells, and basic speech with aquatic creatures.

**Lizardfolk**: Think Argonian. Comes with a swimming speed, enhanced ability to hold your breath, a bite attack, natural armor, and some skill proficiencies.

**Tortle**: Big walking turtle. Comes with enhanced ability to hold your breath, a claw attack, natural armor, a skill proficiency, and the ability to withdraw into your shell. If you want to be based on a sea turtle we can give you a swim speed too.

**Locathah**: Fish on top and humanoid on bottom, the way God intended. Comes with a swim speed, water breathing, natural armor, skill proficiencies, and advantage against a bunch of status conditions.

**Sea Elf**: Elf but blue. Comes with a swimming speed, water breathing, cold resistance, basic speech with sea creatures, and standard elf features

**Warforged**: Not inherently thematic, but wanted to call this one out since they’re not typically present in the Forgotten Realms setting but they will be in this campaign. Feel free to get creative with the materials used in your construction! Comes with no need to breathe, eat, drink, or sleep (but still need rest, not unlike elves), advantage on saving throws against Poisoned and resistance to poison damage, disease immunity, +1 AC, and a skill or tool proficiency
